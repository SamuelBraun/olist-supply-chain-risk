3 / From data to prototypes
04 / 11

Four PySpark prototypes light up four parts of the data network, then converge into one weekly seller watchlist.

Each prototype owns one signal and joins a different subset of the 9 tables. The Seller Risk Index weights them and bands the result.

①
Demand Forecaster
Forecasts each seller's weekly order volume and tracks delivery lateness (Spark MLlib). The lateness signal feeds the risk index; the growth forecast flags sellers outgrowing their delivery capacity.
②
Sentiment Monitor
Reads every Portuguese review and scores customer satisfaction per seller, reliably separating complaints from praise (Spark NLP pipeline plus a PyTorch deep-learning model). Satisfaction is a direct health signal, not a volume predictor.
③
Supply-Network Graph
Maps which sellers share customers (GraphFrames) to find structural single-points-of-failure and name a backup seller for each. This is risk no per-seller dashboard can see.
④
Risk Archetypes
Spark MLlib groups sellers into four failure modes (delivery-driven, satisfaction-driven, structurally-critical, low-risk), so each can get a targeted response.

4 / Why this is a big-data problem
05 / 11

Even at today's volume, the marketplace produces the 4 V's by design, and 8.1% of orders are already late.

Volume · Velocity · Variety · Veracity. All four show up structurally, not because of dataset size.

V1
Volume
~1.5M rows
99,441 orders · 112,650 order-line items · 104,162 reviews · 103,886 payments · 1M raw geo points. Today's dataset spans 2 years; the marketplace produces this every quarter at scale.
V2
Velocity
weekly refresh
New orders, reviews, and delivery events arrive continuously. The Seller Risk Index rescores every seller weekly, running the same code every Monday.
V3
Variety
4 data shapes
Structured orders + Portuguese free-text reviews (43,337 rows) + geographic coordinates (19,015 centroids) + graph relationships (seller↔customer). One Spark pipeline absorbs all four.
V4
Veracity
dirty in → trusted out
Raw marketplace data is incomplete and noisy, and Spark cleans it before any seller is scored: ~2,965 orders with missing delivery dates are dropped (their timing can't be trusted); ~1M scattered geo-points collapse to 19,015 reliable zip-centroids; ambiguous neutral reviews and a tail of extreme delivery outliers are filtered out. The watchlist only ever sees data the client can trust.

6 / What the analysis revealed
07 / 11

Three things a per-seller dashboard cannot tell you.

Each finding comes from combining signals, not from any single seller KPI.

01 · Signal independence
Risk is three separate problems, not one.
Demand pressure, customer sentiment and network criticality barely move together. The strongest correlation between any two is just 0.22. A seller can ship on time, keep customers happy and still be a structural single point of failure. No single KPI catches all three.
02 · Supply-network graph
The biggest sellers aren't the irreplaceable ones.
Ranking sellers by size just re-finds the biggest accounts. Our substitutability signal surfaces small sellers no competitor can cover. They are invisible to a volume dashboard, yet they are the ones whose failure the marketplace cannot absorb. 57 at-risk sellers have no viable backup.
03 · Customer sentiment
Reviews confirm trouble. They don't predict it.
Our model tells happy from unhappy reviews almost perfectly, yet sentiment barely leads order volume (peak |ρ| 0.015). Delivery delay moves first. So Olist should trigger on delays and use sentiment to confirm, never the other way round.
Why we built one number
Because the three signals are independent, no single metric ranks risk on its own. We fold delivery, customer satisfaction and structural importance into one weighted score per seller.

9 / Close & next steps

What we built · what's next.

What we built

  • One reproducible PySpark notebook with visible outputs in every cell, deterministic across re-runs (step cache, 6 parquet outputs)
  • Four prototypes: Demand Forecaster · Sentiment Monitor · Supply-Network Graph · Risk Archetypes
  • One unified deliverable: the Seller Risk Index, a weekly watchlist with archetype tag and recommended action
  • A Structured Streaming prototype that re-expresses the weekly scoring as a live stream (bonus track, delivered)

What's next

  • Productionise on Databricks / EMR as a scheduled weekly Spark job
  • Wire the watchlist into the seller-success CRM, with Slack alerts on new WARNING entries
  • Expand the graph: add category, postal region and shipper as new vertex types
From a list of sellers to a managed network, and from post-complaint discovery to weekly intervention.
