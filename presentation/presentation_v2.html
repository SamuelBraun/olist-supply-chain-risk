3 / From data to prototypes
04 / 10

Four PySpark prototypes light up four parts of the data network, then converge into one weekly seller watchlist.

Each prototype owns one signal — and joins a different subset of the 9 tables. The Seller Risk Index weights them and bands the result.

①
Demand Forecaster
Forecasts each seller's weekly order volume and tracks delivery lateness (Spark MLlib). The lateness signal feeds the risk index; the growth forecast flags sellers outgrowing their delivery capacity.
②
Sentiment Monitor
Reads every Portuguese review and scores customer satisfaction per seller — reliably separating complaints from praise (Spark NLP pipeline + a PyTorch deep-learning model). Satisfaction is a direct health signal, not a volume predictor.
③
Supply-Network Graph
Maps which sellers share customers (GraphFrames) to find structural single-points-of-failure and name a backup seller for each — risk no per-seller dashboard can see.
④
Risk Archetypes
Spark MLlib groups sellers into four failure modes — delivery-driven · satisfaction-driven · structurally-critical · low-risk — so each gets the right intervention.

4 / Why this is a big-data problem
05 / 10

Even at today's volume, the marketplace produces the 4 V's by design — and 8.1% of orders are already late.

Volume · Velocity · Variety · Veracity — all four show up structurally, not because of dataset size.

V1
Volume
~1.5M rows
99,441 orders · 112,650 order-line items · 104,162 reviews · 103,886 payments · 1M raw geo points. Today's dataset spans 2 years; the marketplace produces this every quarter at scale.
V2
Velocity
weekly refresh
New orders, reviews, and delivery events arrive continuously. The Seller Risk Index must rescore every seller weekly — the pipeline runs the same code, every Monday.
V3
Variety
4 data shapes
Structured orders + Portuguese free-text reviews (43,337 rows) + geographic coordinates (19,015 centroids) + graph relationships (seller↔customer). One Spark pipeline absorbs all four.
V4
Veracity
dirty in → trusted out
Raw marketplace data is incomplete and noisy, and Spark cleans it before any seller is scored: ~2,965 orders with missing delivery dates are dropped (their timing can't be trusted); ~1M scattered geo-points collapse to 19,015 reliable zip-centroids; ambiguous neutral reviews and a tail of extreme delivery outliers are filtered out. The watchlist only ever sees data the client can trust.

7 / Insights only the network reveals
08 / 10

Delays predict. Sentiment only confirms — and hub-seller rankings shift under stress.

Two findings that a per-seller dashboard cannot surface.

FINDING 1 · ORDER FLOW
Delivery behaviour predicts. Sentiment only confirms.
Delivery problems are the leading signal — they move first. Customer sentiment barely tracks volume, so it confirms problems after the fact rather than predicting them. We treat delays as the early warning and satisfaction as a separate health gauge.
FINDING 2 · WHOLE NETWORK
Hub sellers shift under stress.
Re-running PageRank on the late-orders subgraph changes who the structural hubs are. The dashboard view never sees this; the graph view does. When delays spike, different sellers become systemically important — so Olist should watch the stress-hubs, not just the everyday top sellers.
